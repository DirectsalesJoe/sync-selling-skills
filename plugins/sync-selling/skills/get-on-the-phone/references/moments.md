# The moments, and what to say in each

Each moment has the message that sets up the call, the opening once they pick up, and the questions. Put every line in the user's own words before it goes; these show the shape.

## 1. An enquiry has come in by email

Don't start pricing from the email. Ring first, with a reason worth three minutes.

Find the reason before you pick up: something missing from what they sent, something that doesn't add up, or a question whose answer changes the price.

> Hi [name], it's [you] from [company]. Thanks for the enquiry on [job], we really appreciate the opportunity. A couple of things came up when I was going through it that I think are worth considering, and I think they'll be of benefit to you. Have you got three minutes so I can run them by you?

Then: "I'll ask a few questions to make sure we spec this in the best way possible for you. Does that sound okay?"

Questions: what's prompted this one, who's done it for them before and how that went, what's gone wrong on a job like this, how they'll decide, what matters beyond price, when they need it by.

## 2. A buyer you've never spoken to

Lead with something they'd pay to receive. The test: given what you know, would they pay for this?

> Hi [name], I'm getting in touch because I can see you're [the specific thing: a build programme, a pipeline notice, a new site]. On [jobs like that], [the problem you've seen, in one line]. I've found a couple of things that might help and written some thoughts down. Would you be dead against a quick conversation so I can walk you through them?

## 3. A connection on LinkedIn or a social channel

Ask a question first, so it reads as a conversation, not a pitch. The offer comes once they reply.

> Hi [name], out of interest, do you do many [type of job]?

> [When they reply] We've just looked at the last [number] [type] jobs we did and found the three things that most often [went over budget / held things up]. Would you mind me sending it across? Happy to walk you through the ones that apply to you in ten minutes, if that's useful.

## 4. A quote or proposal that's gone quiet

First, give them a reason to talk: something you've found, or something that's changed.

> Hi [name], something's come up on [job] that I think is worth you knowing before you decide: [what, in one line]. Have you got three minutes this week so I can run it by you?

If they're still quiet, close for a no. A no is worth more than silence, and people correct you when you assume they've gone.

> Hi [name], I haven't heard back, so I'm assuming [the project's been shelved / it's gone somewhere else]. Might be wrong, but really appreciate the opportunity. Are you still looking at it, or have you gone a different route?

## 5. An account you keep losing, or have never won

Use the next job they send you. Ring first; text only to get the call. The couple of things have to be real.

> Hi [name], thanks for the opportunity. There's a couple of things I wanted to run by you to make sure your quote is perfect. Have you got five minutes today or tomorrow morning?

When you get them, set the frame:

> I really appreciate the opportunity. I'm just conscious that we've priced a few jobs for you and always fallen short, so I wanted to find out more about what you value and what you're looking for in a partner. Is that okay?

Then, in this order:

- Perfect. I guess a good place to start is the jobs we've quoted and not won. Do you mind me asking where our quote fell down compared to the partner that won the job?
- And do you mind if I ask, is it the same firm you generally use?
- [If they say price] Do you mind if I ask, were we miles away or pretty similar?
- Do you always go for the outright cheapest?

Those four tell you whether they have someone they always use, or whether they're genuinely open, so you know whether the next quote is worth your time.

- [When they say no] Okay, that makes sense. What are the things you mainly look for?
- And what are the things at project level you want to avoid?
- If you had the opportunity to design the perfect partner, from quote to delivery, what would you most like to see?

Then book the walkthrough:

> Thanks for that. Given what you've told me, I need to go and put this together, and make sure I've ticked all the boxes you've mentioned. Do you have any objection, once it's done, to a quick call where I walk you through it, just to make sure it covers everything you've talked about today?

## 6. Someone who's pushed back

The softener goes first, so they read the rest off the defensive.

> Sorry, one more thing (feel free to tell me to get back in my box). [The new point, in one line]. Would you be dead against a quick call about it?

## 7. The end of any call

Never hang up without the next call booked.

> On the back of this, I'll put together [what you're making], tailored to what you've told me. There's always something that needs talking through, so I'll walk you through it and you can change anything that doesn't hit the mark. How's [day] at [time]? It'll take fifteen minutes.

## When a question feels too personal

Ask "Do you mind me asking...?" first. If they mind, they'll say so and you've lost nothing. If they ask why, tell them: "It just helps us understand what checks we need to put in place, so what we put forward is right for you."
