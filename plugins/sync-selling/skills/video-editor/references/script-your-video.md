# Script the video before they record it

This is for someone who wants a video and hasn't recorded it yet. The video this was built for answers the questions their buyers ask before they buy, so each one is answered before it can come up late in the sale. The same steps work for any video where one person explains something to the camera.

The aim is a crib, never a script to read out. People who read a script sound like they're reading, and a buyer can hear it. A crib is the question and a few words for each point, so they say it the way they'd say it across a table.

## 1. Get the questions

Ask them for the ten objections or concerns they or their team hear most. If they can't think of ten, ask what a buyer says just before they go quiet, and what they ask on nearly every first call.

## 2. Turn each one into a question a buyer would ask

Write it the way the buyer would say it out loud, in their words. It names the topic and leaves the worry out.

- "It's too expensive" becomes "How much does it cost?"
- "I haven't got time" becomes "How much time will it need from me?"
- "I can do this myself" becomes "Can't I just do this myself?"

Show them the list and let them change the wording. They know how their buyers talk.

## 3. Write a crib for each answer

Each answer goes through four points, in this order:

1. What most people do about this.
2. Why that doesn't work.
3. What we do instead.
4. Why that works, and what the buyer gets from it.

For each question, ask them how they'd answer it if a buyer asked on a call, and take their own words down. Then set those words out under the four points, a few words for each. Use only what they told you. If a point has nothing under it, ask them for it, and never fill it in yourself. Any number or result in an answer has to be one they gave you.

An answer should take about two minutes to say. If a crib runs longer than that, the answer is trying to cover two questions, so split it.

## 4. Tell them how to record it

These four habits are what make the edit easy:

- **Say the question out loud first, then answer it.** The viewer needs to hear what's being answered.
- **When you fluff a line, stop, take a breath, and say the whole sentence again from the start.** The editor keeps the last go at every line, so they never need to stop the recording.
- **Leave a second of quiet between answers.** That gives each answer a clean start.
- **Record it all in one go.** One long recording with the mistakes left in is easier to edit than ten short ones.

A phone or a laptop camera is fine. They should face a window so the light is on their face, put the camera at eye level, and look at the lens. If they film on an iPhone, ask them to switch HDR Video off first (Settings, Camera, Record Video), because HDR recordings can come out looking flat once they're edited.

## 5. Give them the crib

Give them one page: each question, with its four points under it. They can keep it on screen beside the camera while they record. When the recording's done, go back to step 2 of the skill.
